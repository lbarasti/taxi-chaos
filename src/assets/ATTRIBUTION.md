City art combines procedural drawing and self-contained embedded artwork.

`src/assets/characters/` holds the user-supplied PNG portraits for the Open Shore
cast (Mara, Eli, Victor, Celia, Adrian Vale, Leila, Harker, Lena, Iris, Tom, Ruth
and Bea) plus the bundled `officer.png`. They are raster art supplied for Taxi
Chaos and are bundled inline as data URLs with no external image service.

`characters/{sam,june,ravi,cleo,oscar}.png` are original passenger portraits
generated with OpenAI's built-in image-generation tool on 2026-10-22, using the
supplied Bea portrait as a style reference. They were cropped from a contact
sheet and packaged as indexed PNGs. The prompt requested five distinct fictional
adults in the same painterly style, seated in a dark teal taxi's rear cabin with
charcoal leather headrests, a softly blurred coastal city through the windows and
warm afternoon light. These portraits belong to the ordinary customers; Bea's
existing headset portrait remains the dispatch identity. All are bundled inline.

The legacy ids `mia` and `frankie` stay in `src/narrative/characters.json` because the
authored legacy mission catalogue and the dialogue preview reference them. Their
supplied rasters are no longer shipped, so each resolves to its own neutral
placeholder card (`legacy-mia.png`, `legacy-frankie.png`): original flat artwork
created for Taxi Chaos with a featureless silhouette and the initial only,
regenerated by `tools/prepare-legacy-portraits.py`. These cards are not a portrait
of any cast member and never stand in for the removed raster art.

Sound FX attribution, source links, licences and editing notes are in
[`audio/CREDITS.md`](audio/CREDITS.md), embedded in the standalone HTML and
accessible from the game's welcome/pause settings.

## Map artwork

`map/shop-tar.png`, `map/shop-rust.png`, `map/shop-concrete.png`, and
`map/tree-worn.png` were generated for Taxi Chaos with OpenAI's built-in image
generation tool, using a user-supplied urban scene as a style reference. Prompt
direction was straight orthographic overhead, muted weathered materials, blank
runtime sign panels and no baked cast shadows. The three roofs use patched tar,
rusted corrugated metal and aged concrete; the tree uses an irregular olive/sage
canopy. Transparent export margins were trimmed and sprites resized to 256 px
roofs and a 192 px tree. Exact original prompts remain in Git history.
`map/asphalt.svg` is original vector surface detail.
These assets require no external fonts, images or runtime services.

`map/hospital-structure.png` adapts the user-supplied
`hospital-structure-v2.png` for the two-block Midtown Hospital. OpenAI's built-in
image-generation tool extended the roof vertically and retained its weathered
concrete, skylights, pipes and teal helipad. The original upload remains unchanged.
The adaptation requested a 26:66 footprint by adding matching roof sections,
preserving the circular helipad rather than stretching it, removing the red
cutout fringe and leaving a transparent background without external cast shadows.
Exact original prompts remain in Git history; current crop, metre bounds and
light outline are defined in `src/artwork/catalogue.ts`. The PNG is bundled
inline with a procedural fallback.

`map/luxury-condo.png` is OpenAI built-in image-generation option A, adapted from
Lorenzo's supplied condo to retain its cream roof and pool with minimal visible
facade. It is used by Azure Residences. `map/ocean-crest-condo.png` and
`map/pacific-palm-hotel.png` were generated from scratch with the same built-in tool
for Ocean Crest and Pacific Palm. The prompts request orthographic straight-down
roofs, muted weathered materials, minimal visible walls, transparent backgrounds,
no surrounding ground or cast shadows, and no text. Ocean Crest has a slate roof,
terracotta roof terrace and lap pool; Pacific Palm has a long seafoam/cream roof,
HVAC plant, blue skylights and a rooftop pool. The hotel roof was extended in depth
to match its 3:1 footprint. Authored draw-time crops exclude faint alpha specks.
All three sprites are bundled inline with procedural fallbacks.

`map/palm-canopy.png` was regenerated with OpenAI's built-in image-generation tool,
using `map/tree-worn.png` as a style and camera reference. The prompt requests one
orthographic straight-down palm crown, muted olive/sage feathered fronds, weathered
painterly detail, a crown centred over the upright trunk, a transparent background,
and no visible long trunk, ground, text or baked cast shadow. The PNG is bundled
inline; the shared catalogue supplies its trunk collision and runtime sun silhouette.

`map/crate-shipping.png` and `map/bakery-front.png` are original
pixel art created for Taxi Chaos (simple generated artwork, no third-party source) for the
shared catalogue's representative crate and bakery building records. Both are
small sprites bundled inline with the other map artwork.

- `map/grass-{short,lush,clover,daisies,dry,tufts}.svg`: original grass tile artwork created for Taxi Chaos; six matching-edge variants with blades, clover, small daisies and dry tufts.

`map/golf-course.png` was made with OpenAI's built-in image generation in two
steps: a realistic, exactly overhead aerial of a naturally designed three-hole
woodland course, then a ground-only edit of the user-selected first aerial.
The edit preserves the flowing fairways, irregular greens, eight sand bunkers,
cart paths, parking apron and upper-right pond. Its direction explicitly removes
every tree, shrub, rock, building, car, person, flag, furnishing and external
object shadow, replacing vegetation with flat rough and the clubhouse with an
empty paving pad. The background contains ground and water only.

`map/golf-clubhouse.png` was generated separately from that same aerial as a cream
overhead roof sprite. Its packaged crop uses only the opaque main roof, excluding
the export's unwanted halo and projecting wings. Both PNGs use an indexed palette;
the course is packaged at 1280 px wide and the roof at 256 px wide. The real
building body, tree trunks/canopies, furniture, flags, tee markers, people and
carts draw afterwards in their respective prop/actor passes; destruction and
layer visibility never reveal a duplicate painted object underneath.

The authored map records the pond shoreline and matching bunker terrain, plus
ordinary catalogue placements for the clubhouse, trees and furniture. The
previous four course candidates and separate water texture have been removed.
Changed course geometry or a failed decode uses the existing geometric fallback.
These assets are bundled inline and require no external service.


## Daily newspaper photographs

`newspapers/day-01.webp` through `day-14.webp` and the three
`newspapers/investigation-*.webp` pictures are original images generated with the
built-in OpenAI image-generation tool for Taxi Chaos on 2026-10-21. Days 1–7 are
1000 × 667 px; days 8–14 and the investigation pictures are 1024 × 576 px. All are
WebP and embedded in the standalone game, displayed at their natural proportions.
No third-party source photograph was used. Some later scenes were cropped from
generated contact sheets and are intended for newspaper-size display.

The shared prompt requested one landscape black-and-white editorial photograph
of a fictional worn coastal city: natural documentary lighting, subtle newspaper
grain, convincing weathered materials, a clear thumbnail composition, no readable
words, logos, captions, watermarks, invented arrests or election results. Subjects:

- Day 1: maintenance workers repairing a promenade streetlamp at sunrise.
- Day 2: temporary delivery barriers beside a still-open waterfront café.
- Day 3: freshly shattered café windows before the morning boarding work.
- Day 4: boarded café windows beside a cleaner’s broom and bucket.
- Day 5: telephone, proofs and anonymous envelopes on the local editor’s desk.
- Day 6: waiting chairs and a folder outside a municipal hearing building.
- Day 7: an empty patrol parking space beside shuttered harbour shops.
- Day 8: an old radio, booking slips and tea on a taxi dispatch desk.
- Day 9: chairs and empty sign stands outside a modest seaside hotel.
- Day 10: a supplier register and carbon copies at a public records counter.
- Day 11: working boats, crates and workshop doors on the quay.
- Day 12: a suitcase near temporary lodging, without identifying a source’s destination.
- Day 13: a printing press feeding a fresh stack of newspapers.
- Day 14: a polling-station entrance before opening, without an election result.
- Café investigation: a shuttered café threshold and barriers.
- Patrol investigation: a reassignment log beside a station radio.
- Vale investigation: a briefing folder and retained supplier agreement.
